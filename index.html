Transition credit risk engine

An open, reproducible engine that translates NGFS Phase V transition scenarios into probabilities of default and expected loss for a non-financial corporate loan book, by NACE sector, for Italy and Germany, 2025 to 2050.

Channels follow the ECB economy-wide climate stress test (Occasional Paper 281): carbon cost on Scope 1 emissions, sector abatement, GDP.

NGFS scenarios to corporate PDs

64,000 firms simulated from BACH company-account quartiles. Merton model calibrated on Pillar 3 EU CR9 default rates.

Finding 1

Risk peaks in 2042, then falls.

Carbon prices rise faster than sector emissions fall until the early 2040s. Afterwards decarbonisation shrinks the base the price applies to.

+4.96 pp

Peak change in the Italian portfolio PD under Net Zero 2050 versus Current Policies. Germany peaks at +1.44 pp, also in 2042.

Finding 2

Risk is concentrated.

In 2040 the largest shifts sit in agriculture, Italian waste management and transport. Manufacturing moves +2 to +4 pp.

Dispersion matters too: thin-margin firms cross the default threshold first. Firm-level data gives +2.66 pp for Italy in 2030, against +0.53 pp with one representative firm per industry.

Finding 3

One assumption drives Italy's 2050 figure.

If agricultural CH₄ and N₂O are exempt from the carbon price, the Italian 2050 shift falls from +2.77 pp to +0.63 pp.

Scenario-model choice moves it by up to 0.9 pp; the leverage cap and asset volatility by less than 0.2 pp.

Why it matters

Built for the 2027 guidelines.

EBA/GL/2025/04 on environmental scenario analysis apply from 1 January 2027 and require banks to integrate environmental risks into stress testing.

Every input is public and cited, every parameter sits in one configuration file, and every run writes a manifest with the hash of each input.
